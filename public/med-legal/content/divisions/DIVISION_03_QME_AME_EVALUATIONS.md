## Chapter 29: Evaluation By Treating Physician

When the medical treatment condition becomes permanent and
stationary the primary treating physician renders a P&S report. See
last section of Treatment chapter for further discussion of
“Permanent And Stationary.”

Reports of the treating physician are admissible.

LC§4060(b), 5703(a)

Release-From-Treatment Report

This is not a comprehensive medical legal report. It is a report on
PR-3 form.

Treating physician’s reports do not have to comply with LC §§
4061/4062.

The Spice Hunter v. WCAB 60 CCC 655 (wd);
Hunt-Wesson Foods v. WCAB 63 CCC 85
(wd)

Reports of treating physician in denied claim need not comply with
R§9785.

Golden Gate Bridge District v. WCAB 66
CCC 1362.

Notice to Employee When TTD Ends

Together with the last payment of temporary disability the employer
shall notify the employee of one of the following:

(1) No permanent disability will be paid; or

(2) The amount of permanent disability that will be paid, or

(3) The condition is not yet permanent and stationary.

LC§4061(a)

If the condition is not yet permanent and stationary but the
employee can return to regular or modified duty, the employer shall
monitor the medical condition until the condition becomes
permanent and stationary.

The notice shall describe what procedures are available to the
employee if the employee does not agree with the employer’s rating
of the disability..

LC§4061(b)

App not obligated to object to medical report when notice of time
limit to do so was sent in English to a monolingual Spanish speaking
IW. AD Rule 9810(i) requires all benefit notices to be available in
English or Spanish, as appropriate.

Vargas v. TIG Ins. Co. (2009) 37 CWCR 122
(WCAB)

Medical — Legal Evaluation by Treating Physician

Either party may request a comprehensive medical legal report from
the treating physician. No medical legal reports are admissible
except for those by the treating physician and the report of the
AME/QME for represented employees and the QME panel by
unrepresented employees.

LC§4060(a),

“The report is obtained at the request of a party ... for the purpose of
proving or disproving a contested claim and addresses the disputed
medical fact or facts specified by the party, or parties or other person
who requested the comprehensive medical-legal evaluation report.”

subsections | thru 5.

If there is more than one treating physician because of more than
one specialty required then applicant must be P&S in all specialties.

May get reports in other specialties but only one report is allowed
which incorporates or comments on all other specialty reports.

LC 4062.2

QME procedures in denied case to determine compensability -
If a medical evaluation is required to determine compensability then

LC 4060(c)

Ordinarily, the process of evaluation an employee’s permanent
disability does not start until the person’s condition has become
permanent and stationary. Usually this is when treatment is
completed. However, def may object to PTP opinion that condition
not p&s, and then commence the panel QME process.

P&S Permanent and stationary is the same as Maximum Medical
Improvement (MMI) as defined in the AMA Guides.

“An impairment is considered permanent when it has reached
maximal medical improvement (MMI), meaning it is well stabilized
and unlikely to change substantially in the next year with or without
medical treatment.”

Guides page 2

Primary treating physician issues a P&S report.

4061.5. The treating physician primarily responsible for managing
the care of the injured worker or the physician designated by that
treating physician shall, in accordance with rules promulgated by the
administrative director, render opinions on all medical issues
necessary to determine eligibility for compensation. In the event
that there is more than one treating physician, a single report shall
be prepared by the physician primarily responsible for managing the
injured worker's care that incorporates the findings of the various
treating physicians.

The treating physician’s report can be on DWC form PR-4
http://www.dir.ca.gov/dwe/PR-4.pdf

The parties may choose to accept the findings and rating of the
treating physician’s report. If a party chooses not to accept the
findings and opinions in the report the party must object to the
report.

These procedures apply only to an admitted claim. An admitted
claim is where the injury to any body part is admitted.

What happens when treating physician’s opinions are not
accepted?

The parties must follow the procedures of LC 4062.2 when the
employee is represented. Parties could stipulate to not follow 4062.2
and to each get their own QME and not to object to the other’s QME

WC physical medical examination and can record by audiotape. An
attorney’s observer must be authorized in writing. Observer can
audiotape record. If the recording is to be used in evidence it must
be provided to the other side.

CCP§2032.510(a), LC§4052; Fireman's Fund
Ins. Co. v. WCAB (Landeros) 45 CCC 37 (LC
4052 not limited to physicians), Stephens v.
WCAB 64 CCC 287 (wd) (tape must be
provided to defense if might be used at trial),
Penman v. WCAB 60 CCC 793 (WCJ did not
permit applicant’s attorney to attend
examination.)

The observer can stop the exam if examiner becomes abusive to the
examinee or undertakes to engage in unauthorized diagnostic tests.
Same rights in CCP apply in WC.

Supra

CCP does not require notice that the examination will be recorded
but counsel may wish to consider giving notice and prominently
displaying the recording device. There is a prohibition against
surreptitiously recording the examination.

PC §632; Flanagan v. Flanagan 27 Cal. re
766 (Cal Supreme Court overruled OLaskey v.
Sortino. Any surreptitious recording of
conversation is violation of privacy.)

Defense counsel is not authorized to record a physical examination.

Videotaping of physical examination is not permitted.

Ramirez v. MacAdam (1993) 13 CA4th 1638,
16 CR2d 911

Mental Examination

Both examiner and examinee have the choice of recording the
mental examination on audiotape. It is recommended that this be
done. Videotaping is not permitted.

CCP§2032.530(a), Vinson v. Superior Court
(1987) 43 C3d 833, 239 CR 292, Brewer v.
WCAB (np) 51 CCC 190

Attorney or observer is not permitted at a mental examination except
by order from board.

Edwards v. Superior Court 16 C3d 905, 130
CR 14; CCP§2032.530(b)

Contesting AME Opinion

If the report is not submitted within 30 days of the appointment
without good cause it is late. Can a party withdraw from the
agreement or contest the report because it is late? LC 4062.5 says
that a party can get a new panel if the selected QME’s report is late,
but an AMEB’s report is not mentioned in 4062.5 even though an
AME is mentioned in Reg §38(a).

LC§4062.5; R§38(a)

Other possible reasons to contest the report are: improper
communication with the AME or AME presented evidence not
agreed upon by the parties (surveillance video).

See above procedures.

Evidentiary Weight of AME’s Opinion

AME opinion is entitled to great weight and usually must be
followed by WCJ unless WCJ states good reasons not to follow the
AME.

Power v. WCAB 179 CA3d 775, 224 CR 758,
51 CCC 114; Siqueiros v. WCAB 60 CCC
150; Rashidizand v. Bijan, Inc. (1990) 19
CWCR 204

AME report rejected because not substantial evidence.

Anderson v. University of Calif. (2000) 28
CWCR 335; Santa Clara Co. Transit Dist. v.
WCAB 60 CCC 148 (AME had no coherent
rationale)

Finding of industrial injury to sinuses from mold exposure upheld
based on opinion of PTP and contrary to opinion of AME.

County of Sacramento v. WCAB (Smith)
(2012) 78 CCC 45 (CA 3'— wd)

WCJ may determine PD by using portions of different medical
reports. For example, using AME opinion for knee impairment and
PTP opinion for low back impairment.

NBC Universial Media v. WCAB (Moussa)
(2014) 79 CCC 191 (CA 2" - wd)

WCJ can find higher disability than AME.

Warren v. Bank of America (1998) 17 CWCR
103

TD award based on psych condition reversed because AME
diagnosed condition before seeing relevant medical records.

Radiator USA v. WCAB (Kang) (2015) 80
CCC 79, 43 CWCR 29 (CA 2"—nonpub)
