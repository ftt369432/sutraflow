## Chapter 47: Deposition

Discovery is Done by Deposition

CCP§2004 (definition)

The only formal discovery procedure in a worker’s compensation case
is the deposition.

Lubin v. WCAB 41 CCC 283; Moran v.
WCAB 57 CCC 273; cf. Hardesty v. WCAB
41 CCC 111 (“We wish to make it clear,

3, that the procedural provisions of the

“discovery”. Depositions do apply.)

Discovery can be commenced after the application is filed — board has
| jurisdiction. (for injuries after 1/1/94)

(1994) (AD) 9786-2105, 23 CWCR 17

Subpoenas can only be served after the claim form has been filed and
the application filed so that the WCAB has jurisdiction. (Except in
window period 1/1/90 — 1/1/94)

R§10530, Assogna v. API Appliance Parts,
Inc. (1994) (AD) 9786-2105,23 CWCR 17
Former LC§5401

In order for the defense to conduct discovery, i.e. take the deposition of
the applicant, or of a doctor or subpoena records of an unrepresented
employee, the defense must file an application for adjudication. If the
defense does so, the entire attorney’s fee for the employee must be paid
by the employer.

LC§4064(b); Lompoc Unified School
District v. WCAB 64 CCC 105 (wd)

Use of the deposition is preferred over informal discovery through the
attorney because materials must be produced timely and the problems
of materials not produced is reduced, i.e. documents removed, files
sanitized, documents lost or misplaced or documents not received by
the attorney (“We gave you everything we have.”)

LC§5710 says that depositions may be taken as in civil actions in the
superior court. “To that end the attendance of witnesses and the
production of records may be required.” Depositions are taken in
superior court under CCP§2020.010 for nonparty deponents and
CCP§2025.010 for party deponents.

LC§5710, CCP§2025.280

In 1998 LC§5710 was amended. To the sentence that depositions are
taken in the manner prescribed by law for like depositions in civil
actions in the superior courts of this state, was added, “under Article 3
(commencing with Section 2016) of Chapter 3 of Title 4 of Part 4 of
the Code of Civil Procedure.” The effect of this amendment is to
make it clear that depositions are taken via 2016.010 et seq and not
through R§10530 and 10532 and CCP§1987 and 1987.5.

any video exists before the deposition of applicant.

Downing v. City of Hayward (1988) 16

Defendant need not disclose video or film before the deposition.
Applicant’s attorney can condition deposition on a stipulation that
defendant will produce a copy of any and all video within 10 days of
the completion of the deposition.

City of LA v. WCAB 59 CCC 1062

But, if the video has been disclosed to a physician, defendant must
disclose the video to applicant prior to his or her deposition.

Nisle v. Albertsons, Inc. (2000) 28 CWCR
299

Surveillance video is not within attorney-client privilege and is
discoverable.

Suezaki v. Superior Court 58 C2d 166, 23 CR
368

Videos are not attorney work product.

In Re Alleged Contempt of American Motorist
Inc. and Harold Schmidt 41 CCC 95

But once it has been disclosed that there are videos of applicant,
defendant must disclose it before the deposition.

Nisle v. Albertsons, Inc. (2000) 28 CWCR
