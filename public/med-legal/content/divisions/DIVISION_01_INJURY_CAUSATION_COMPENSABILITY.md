## Chapter 9: Cumulative Or Continuous Trauma (Ct)

There are two types of injuries in workers compensation:

An injury may be either:

(a) “specific” is an injury occurring as the result of one incident or
exposure which causes disability or need for medical treatment;
(b) “cumulative” can be either a continuous trauma or an
occupational disease. A continuous trauma (CT) is an injury
occurring as repetitive mental or physical traumatic activities
extending over a period of time, the combined effect of which
causes disability and/or need for medical treatment. An
occupational disease causes a disability and/or need for medical
treatment that arises as the result of an exposure extending over a
period of time to a disease-producing hazardous substance such as
exposure to toxic substances or asbestos.

The date of a cumulative injury or occupational disease shall be the
date determined under Section 5412.

LC§3208.1; Western Growers Ins. Co. v.
WCAB (Austin) 16 CA4th 227, 58 CCC 323,
21 CWCR 153

CT is unique to workers’ compensation. An applicant may want to
consider filing an application for adjudication for CT if evaluating
physician attributes disability to a CT or if worker had disability
due to CT along with a specific injury.

Cumulative means occurring as repetitive mentally or physically
traumatic activities extending over a period of time, the combined
effect of which causes any disability or need for medical treatment.

LC§3208.1

A CT can consist of multiple specific injuries.

Aetna Causualty Surety Co. v. WCAB
(Coltharp) 38 CCC 720; Morris v. Ins. Co. of
the West (2002) 68 CCC 778, 30 CWCR 304
(football player files CT -- no medical
evidence of permanent disability resulting
from any one separate specific incidents.)

If there is any doubt as to whether the injury is a specific injury or
a cumulative trauma -- file an application for both and let the
evaluating physicians decide. The physicians may apportion to

| specific and cumulative trauma.

Where a CT is based upon multiple injuries the dollar value of the
award is based on the law in effect on the date of the last injury.

Nuelle v. WCAB (1979) 44 CCC 439

If an application has been filed with only a specific date, it is best
to amend the application to a CT. But if it wasn’t amended by the
time of MSC, the date of injury should be amended and listed as an
issue. If it was not amended by the time of trial, the WCJ should
amend according to proof.

R§10492, Blanchard v. WCAB 40 CCC 784;
Beckstead v. WCAB 62 CCC 1646

because the claim form has a single blank for the date of injury.
Employees often only put a single date in the blank. If an
application for adjudication is filed with only a single date the
application should be amended. Another application should not be
filed if the statute of limitations has expired. The amendment is
not a new claim as a new and separate cause of action is not

## Chapter 15: Treatment Is Provided By Employer

Medical treatment “that is reasonably required to cure or
relieve the injured worker from the effects of his or her injury
shall be provided by the employer.” From the Constitution,
“...full provision for such medical, surgical, hospital and
other remedial treatment as is requisite to cure and relieve
from the effects of such injury”

California Constitution, Article XIV, Labor Relations,
§ 4; LC§4600; Turi v. United Airlines (2001) 29
CWCR 126 (worker with retrograde ejaculation
entitled to have sperm removed but not entitled to vitro
fertilization for wife. See ed. note.) For same result,
see: Croushorn v. WCAB (2011) 76 CCC 674 (CA 4"
—wd)

“Medical treatment which is intended only to relieve, but not
cure, the effects of an industrial injury is appropriate under
section 4600.” “This is true in cases of chronic conditions
where a cure is not possible, but where relief of symptoms is
essential for continued function, as well as in cases involving
the loss of limbs, or other body parts where there is a need for
relief of symptoms.” Cure and relieve is the same as cure or
relieve. Treatment and medication that will only relieve the
effects of an injury but will not cure is appropriate. Treatment
that is palliative only is appropriate. Examples are pain
medication, chiropractic treatment, hormone replacement
therapy and housekeeping services.

Grom is significant because it was decided after SB 899’s
change to “cure and relieve” in 4604.5, and yet the Board held
the change to be, in effect, irrelevant.

Dept. of Corrections v. WCAB (Rowan)(1997) 62 CCC
353 (wd); Grom v SCIF 69 CCC 1567, 33 CWCR 13
(2004) (significant panel decision) (prescription for
cream for low sex hormone levels)

In the case of the employer’s neglect or refusal reasonably to
provide treatment, the employer is liable for the reasonable
expense incurred by or on behalf of the employee in providing
the treatment.
