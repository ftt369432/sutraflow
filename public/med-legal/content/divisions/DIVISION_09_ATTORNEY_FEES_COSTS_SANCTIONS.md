Attorney’s fee is subtracted from employee’s award or settlement. LC§4903(a)
This should have been made clear to employee at first meeting so

that there is no misunderstanding at settlement time or when check is

received.

## Chapter 81: Substitution Of Attorney

Out as attorney of record.

A client may substitute out an attorney of record with or without
cause. Attorney is entitled to quantum merit for services but this
cannot be determined until the end of the case. This does not need a
judge’s approval.

Form 36, R§10774; Fracasse v. Brent 6 C.3d
784 (Supreme Court case)

If a new attorney is taking the case the new attorney should sign a
substitution of attorney and send it to the former attorney along a
letter requesting the file. The former attorney is responsible for time
limits and responding to any documents requiring a response until
the substitution of attorney is filed with the board. Therefore, the
former attorney should make sure that the substitution of attorney is
signed by the client and the new attorney and is filed with the board.

In re White & Bunch 46 CCC 810

Former attorney should file and serve a lien for attorney’s fee.

Filing of lien not necessary but good idea.

SCIF v. WCAB (Chester) (1971) 36 CCC 678

If applicant is being substituted in as pro per have them sign CYA
letter re any S/L such as 132a, S&W, third party case and FEHA.

If the attorney wants to terminate his or her representation and the
client does not agree to attorney substituting out, the attorney must
file petition and get order from WCJ. WCJ will not automatically
approve an attorney substituting out. There must be a valid reason,
but the attorney need not give specifics as it would be a breach of
confidentiality.

Deterioration of attorney-client relationship justifies attorney=s

w/drawal; disclosure of reasons would violate attorney-client
privilege.

Desmuke v. Majestic, Ins. (2008) 36 CWCR
115 (WCAB)

In as attorney of record:

If a former attorney does not sign substitution form can use
dismissal of attorney form.

CCP §284; Messih v. Lee Drug, Inc. 174
CA3d 312

Fee splitting between the former attorney and the attorney that
completed the case is usually by agreement. Usually it is 25% to the
former attorney unless there has been substantial work done by the
final attorney then the fee may be split by the amount of time each
attorney represented the client. If there has been a written settlement
offer made during the time the former attorney was attorney of
record then the former attorney’s fee can be based upon that offer.

If there is a dispute as to attorney’s fee division the board should
handle it as any other lien, i.e. follow lien procedures.

Reed v. Travelers Inc. Co. (1998) 26 CWCR.
138
