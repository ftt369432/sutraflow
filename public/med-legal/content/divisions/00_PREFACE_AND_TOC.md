### 🚨 LANDMARK 2025 CONTROLLING PRECEDENT: *DPR CONSTRUCTION V. WCAB (MCCLANAHAN)* (2025)
*   **Official Citation:** *DPR Construction et al. v. Workers' Comp. Appeals Bd. (McClanahan)* (2025) ___ Cal.App.5th ___ [2025 Cal. App. LEXIS 378, Certified for Publication June 11, 2025, No. C102117]
*   **Controlling Statutory Mandate:** Labor Code § 5502(d)(3) & 8 CCR § 10670 [formerly 8 CCR § 10622]
*   **The Supreme Procedural Holding:**
    1. **Strict Discovery Cutoff at MSC:** Discovery strictly closes on the date of the Mandatory Settlement Conference (MSC). Evidence not disclosed or obtained thereafter is **inadmissible** unless the proponent demonstrates it was unavailable despite due diligence.
    2. **NO HARMLESS ERROR ANALYSIS:** Disregard of Section 5502(d)(3)'s procedural bar is **NOT subject to harmless error review**.
    3. **Rejection of "No Prejudice" Defense:** The Court of Appeal explicitly rejected the carrier's argument that admitting late evidence was excused because the opposing party suffered "no prejudice" or had seen the records before. Allowing unlisted evidence on a "no prejudice" rationale would render Section 5502(d)(3) meaningless.
    4. **Excess of Jurisdiction:** When a statute requires a tribunal to exercise its jurisdiction in a particular manner, admitting unlisted evidence beyond those limits is an act **in excess of the Appeals Board's powers and jurisdiction** (*Burtnett v. King* (1949) 33 Cal.2d 805, 807).

> [!CAUTION]
> **HOW TO EXECUTE THE DPR CONSTRUCTION HAMMER AT TRIAL:**  
> If defense counsel attempts to introduce unlisted medical records, MPN notice letters, or late investigation dumps on the day of trial:
> 1. Object immediately on the record under Labor Code § 5502(d)(3) and 8 CCR § 10670.
> 2. Cite *DPR Construction v. WCAB (McClanahan)* (2025) and *San Bernardino Community Hospital v. WCAB (McKenzie)* (1999) 74 Cal.App.4th 928, 938.
> 3. State clearly: *"Admission of this unlisted exhibit exceeds the Board's jurisdiction and is reversible error not subject to harmless error analysis. Opposing counsel cannot cure this defect by claiming lack of prejudice."*
